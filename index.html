LINKS 2027

Language Models, Information, Networks, Knowledge, and Society

Working across disciplines to understand and improve how language models are reshaping the information environment, public discourse, and society.

About

Large language models are increasingly embedded in search engines, social platforms, and conversational assistants, where they shape how people ask questions, choose sources, interpret evidence, form opinions, and take part in public life. They may lower barriers to information access, help people synthesize complex material, support deliberation, and provide tailored explanations, but they can also generate misinformation, reproduce cultural and ideological biases, foster over-reliance, and obscure how information was selected or transformed. These effects extend beyond individual conversations: LLM outputs are shared, remixed, and amplified across social networks, and AI-generated content increasingly populates the feeds and search results that others encounter.

Research on these effects spans HCI, the social sciences, natural language processing, and policy, but these communities often rely on different methods, concepts, and standards of evidence. This workshop uses the LLM-mediated information environment as an integrating frame to bring these communities together, build shared concepts, compare methods, and develop a human-centered research agenda for studying how LLMs shape what people know, believe, and discuss.

Themes & Topics

The workshop invites work across four overlapping themes:

  • Information seeking, sensemaking, and epistemic agency
  • Trust, reliance, persuasion, and human–AI interaction
  • Civic discourse, political bias, misinformation, social media, and moderation
  • Human-centered evaluation, auditing, governance, and policy

We welcome empirical, technical, qualitative, theoretical, design-oriented, audit-based, and policy work, with particular interest in contributions that connect multiple levels of analysis.

Submissions

We invite two-page position papers in ACM single-column format, as well as an alternative 500-word statement of interest for practitioners and community members for whom a formal position paper would create an unnecessary barrier. Submissions will be lightly reviewed for relevance, clarity of perspective, and contribution to disciplinary and methodological diversity. The submission link will be posted here soon.

The workshop is non-archival. With author permission, accepted submissions will be posted on the workshop website; there will be no formal proceedings. This allows participants to discuss preliminary, ongoing, or previously published work while preserving future publication options. At least one author of each accepted position paper will be required to attend the workshop in person.
